The Meaning Check

Do This Before Publishing Any Visual

Read the visual as a sentence: "There are [number] [thing]." or "This chart shows [what] broken down by [how]."

If the sentence is wrong — fix the label or the calculation. If the sentence is confusing — simplify.

Chart Selection Guide

If you want to answer…Use this chart
Who has the most?Bar chart
What percentage of the total?Pie or donut chart
How did this change over time?Line chart
What are the exact numbers?Table
What is the single most important number?Card visual

Column Types

TypeWhat it containsWhat Power BI does with itExample
Category (C)Labels, names, groupsGroups and filtersRank, Unit, Status
Number (N)Measurements, quantitiesCounts, sums, averagesBudget, Days, Score
Watch Out

A number is not always a number column. Unit numbers like "129" and "144" are categories — they label, they don't measure. If you wouldn't add two values together and expect something meaningful, it's a category.

Publishing Checklist

  1. ✓Every visual has a plain English label
  2. ✓Most important information is at the top left
  3. ✓At least one slicer is visible and functional
  4. ✓Meaning check passed on every card
  5. ✓Shown to one person who asked "what does this tell you?"
  6. ✓Published to Power BI Service (not the .pbix file)
  7. ✓Share message tells the reader what question to ask
