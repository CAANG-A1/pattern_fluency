Answer Your First Question

Before This Lesson
Power BI is a complicated tool I have to learn.
→
After This Lesson
Power BI is how I answer questions I already have.

Welcome

For the next 30 minutes, forget Power BI. Forget dashboards, data models, analytics, and every intimidating word you've probably heard attached to this software.

Today we're doing one thing: You're going to answer a question you actually have. Not a practice question. Not a made-up scenario. A real question about something you already work with.

If you can do that, you've succeeded.

Before We Open Anything

I need to ask you three things. Take a minute with each one.

Question 1 — Your Data

What do you already work with? Personnel data. Training records. Budget numbers. Supply inventory. Something you look at regularly. Pick one.

Question 2 — Your Question

What's a question you've wanted to answer about it? Which unit has the most people? Who hasn't completed their training? Where is most of the budget going? Write it down. That question is the engine of this entire lesson.

Question 3 — Your Feeling

How do you feel about Power BI right now? Intimidated? Curious? Skeptical? There is no wrong answer. Notice how you feel now — and notice how you feel at the end.

The Parking Lot

Most training courses start with giant datasets. Think about learning to drive — you don't start on a crowded freeway. You start in an empty parking lot. For the next 20 minutes, this is your parking lot.

Step 1 — Build Your Starting Data

Open Excel. Create a small version of the data you already work with. If you work with personnel data, something like this:

NameUnit
Smith129
Jones144
Brown129
Garcia144
Miller129

If you work with something else, build that instead. Five to ten rows. Two or three columns. The columns that relate to your question. Save it. Name it something you'll recognize.

Why Your Data?

Because you already care about it. When a piano student learns a song they love, they practice longer, remember it faster, and connect to it differently than a student learning an assigned exercise. Your data is your song.

Step 2 — Open Power BI

Launch Power BI Desktop. Look at the screen. There is a lot happening. That's normal. Ignore almost everything. Today we are using a tiny fraction of what Power BI can do.

Step 3 — Bring In Your Data

Step by Step

Click Get Data at the top of the screen.

Choose Excel Workbook.

Find your file. Open it. Check the box next to your sheet. Click Load. Wait.

Stop and Think — What Just Happened?

Power BI copied your data into its workspace. That's it. No analysis. No charts. No insights. It simply brought your information inside.

Think of it like a new employee showing up on day one. They've arrived. They haven't done anything yet. But now you can put them to work.

Step 4 — Build the Chart

Look to the right side of the screen. You should see the column names from your spreadsheet. Click somewhere in the blank white area. Find the chart icon that looks like vertical bars. Click it. An empty chart appears.

Now drag one of your columns — the one related to your question — into the chart. Let go. Look at what happened.

The Moment

Did you write a formula? No. Did you tell Power BI to count anything? No. Power BI looked at your data and summarized it automatically. Now answer the question you wrote down at the beginning. The chart is the vehicle. The answer is the destination. Never mix those up.

Drill #1

▶ Three Times Through

Delete the chart. Build it again. Delete it. Build it again. Delete it. Build it again.

Don't rush. This is the equivalent of practicing scales. We are teaching your hands what success feels like.

Reflection

  1. 01What did Power BI do automatically that surprised you?
  2. 02What did you have to do yourself?
  3. 03Does the chart answer the question you started with?
  4. 04How does this feel compared to looking at the raw spreadsheet?
  5. 05Could you build that chart again without looking at these instructions?

Challenge

Pick a different column from your data. Build a chart for it. Ask yourself: what question does this chart answer? Write the question down after you build the chart — not before. No instructions. Just do it.

Teach Back

Pretend your coworker is standing next to you. They've never opened Power BI. Explain what you did, what Power BI did automatically, what question your chart answers, and why that matters. Plain language only. No technical terms.

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1 — Starting with the tool instead of the question

People click around until something looks right. That's decoration, not analysis. Always start with the question.

Mistake 2 — Using data you don't care about

You'll learn faster and remember longer when the data means something to you. Sample data is the last resort.

Mistake 3 — Skipping the repetition

Three times feels unnecessary. It isn't. Your brain needs the motion to feel routine before it can feel easy.

Mistake 4 — Moving on while confused

If the chart didn't appear, stop here. Do not proceed to Lesson 2. Fix it first. Confusion carried forward becomes a wall.

Homework

Find a spreadsheet you use at work or at home. Import it into Power BI. Build one chart. Answer one question. Don't follow steps. You already know how.

Lesson Summary

Before this lesson, Power BI was a complicated tool you had to learn.

After this lesson, Power BI is a way to answer questions you already have.
