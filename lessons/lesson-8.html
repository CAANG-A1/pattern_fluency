Capstone — Build a Report That Matters

Before This Lesson
I've learned the tools. Now what?
→
After This Lesson
I can take any question my unit has and build a report that answers it.

This Is Not a New Lesson

This is where everything you've learned becomes one thing.

You're going to build a complete report from scratch.

No step-by-step instructions.

No example data.

Your data.

Your question.

Your audience.

Your report.

Then you're going to share it and present it.

This is your graduation from Volume 1.

What You Already Know

  • 01Pattern #1 — Group & Count: Count records by category.
  • 02Pattern #2 — Change the Lens: Choose the visual that answers the question most clearly.
  • 03Pattern #3 — Read the Shape: Understand what questions the data can and cannot answer.
  • 04Pattern #4 — Filter = New Question: Focus a broad question into a specific question.
  • 05Pattern #5 — One Number Answer: Use a card when one number answers the most important question.
  • 06Pattern #6 — Design for the Reader: Make the report understandable without standing there to explain it.
  • 07Pattern #7 — Publish, Test, Improve: Share the report, test whether it communicates, and improve it.
The Shift

You did not learn seven disconnected skills.

You learned seven patterns.

Today, you will connect them into one complete loop.

The Capstone Requirements

Requirement 1 — Real Data

Your data.

Something you or your unit actually works with.

No sample data.

No invented scenarios.

Requirement 2 — A Real Question

A question your unit actually needs answered.

Not a demonstration question.

A question that, if answered clearly, would change a decision or save someone time.

Requirement 3 — A Real Audience

A specific person or group who would benefit from seeing this report.

Not "leadership in general."

Someone specific.

Requirement 4 — Complete Construction

Your report must include:

  • At least two chart visuals
  • At least two card visuals
  • At least one filter or slicer
  • Plain-English labels on every visual
  • A clear arrangement with the most important information at the top
Requirement 5 — Published and Shared

Your report must be published to Power BI Service and shared with your intended audience before your presentation.

Requirement 6 — A Verbal Presentation

Present this report to someone — a coworker, a supervisor, or a classmate.

Five minutes maximum.

No slides.

Just the report on the screen and your voice.

You should be able to explain:

  • What question the report answers
  • How to use the slicer
  • What the most important number is and why
  • What you would add if you had more time
What You Are About to Do

You may think the capstone is about proving you learned Power BI.

It isn't.

The capstone proves you can move through the full Pattern Fluency loop.

You can start with a real question, understand the data, build the answer, design it for someone else, publish it, test it, and improve it.

Pattern #8

Build the Full Loop

Question: Can I turn a real question into a useful report for a real audience?

Recipe:

  • Write the question.
  • Read the shape of the data.
  • Choose visuals that answer the question.
  • Use cards for the most important numbers.
  • Use filters or slicers to focus the answer.
  • Design for the reader.
  • Publish, test, and improve.
  • Present the result.

This is the full loop.

This is what makes the report matter.

How to Approach This

▶ Seven Steps

1 — Write the question. One sentence. Plain English. Before you open Power BI.

2 — Check your data. Does your data support that question? Use Pattern #3.

3 — Design before you build. Sketch what you want on paper. Where will the cards go? The charts? The slicer?

4 — Build. Open Power BI. Build your design. Don't start over every time something isn't perfect — make decisions and move forward.

5 — Test with the meaning check. For every visual, read it as a sentence. If the sentence is wrong, fix it.

6 — Show someone before you present. Ask: "What does this tell you?" Make one adjustment based on their answer.

7 — Publish and present. Share the report and explain what it answers.

Final Validation

Before presenting, check the report like a user, not like the builder.

  • Every chart answers a specific question.
  • Every card reads clearly as a sentence.
  • Every visual has a plain-English title.
  • The slicer works.
  • The report is published.
  • Someone else has tested it.

The Presentation

Your presentation should answer four questions:

  • 01What problem does this report solve?
  • 02What does the most important number on the page mean — and why should anyone care?
  • 03How does a reader use the slicer to focus on what matters to them?
  • 04What would you build next if you had more time?

Five minutes maximum.

No slides.

Just the report and your voice.

Reflection After the Presentation

  • 01What question did someone ask that you weren't prepared for?
  • 02What would you change about the report now that you've seen someone else use it?
  • 03What was the most useful thing on the page?
  • 04What was the least useful?
  • 05What question does your unit have that this report still can't answer?
