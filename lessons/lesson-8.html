Capstone — Build a Report That Matters

Before This Lesson
I've learned the tools. Now what?
→
After This Lesson
I can take any question my unit has and build a report that answers it.

This Is Not a New Lesson

This is where everything you've learned becomes one thing. You're going to build a complete report from scratch. No step-by-step instructions. No example data. Your data. Your question. Your audience. Your report. Then you're going to share it and present it.

This is your graduation from Volume 1.

What You Already Know

  1. 01Lesson 1: Import data and build a chart that answers a question.
  2. 02Lesson 2: Choose the right chart type for the question you're asking.
  3. 03Lesson 3: Read the shape of a spreadsheet and know what questions it can and can't answer.
  4. 04Lesson 4: Use filters to change the focus of a question.
  5. 05Lesson 5: Build card visuals that communicate the most important numbers immediately.
  6. 06Lesson 6: Design a report for a specific person — with clear labels, intentional layout, useful color, and a slicer.
  7. 07Lesson 7: Publish and share a report, and find out whether it worked.

That is a complete toolkit. Now you're going to use all of it at once.

The Capstone Requirements

Requirement 1 — Real Data

Your data. Something you or your unit actually works with. No sample data. No invented scenarios.

Requirement 2 — A Real Question

A question your unit actually needs answered. Not a demonstration question. A question that, if answered clearly, would change a decision or save someone time.

Requirement 3 — A Real Audience

A specific person or group who would benefit from seeing this report. Not "leadership in general." Someone specific.

Requirement 4 — Complete Construction

Your report must include: at least two chart visuals, at least two card visuals, at least one filter or slicer, plain English labels on every visual, and a clear arrangement with the most important information at the top.

Requirement 5 — Published and Shared

Your report must be published to Power BI Service and shared with your intended audience before your presentation.

Requirement 6 — A Verbal Presentation

Present this report to someone — a coworker, a supervisor, a classmate. Five minutes maximum. No slides. Just the report on the screen and your voice. You should be able to explain: what question the report answers, how to use the slicer, what the most important number is and why, and what you would add if you had more time.

How to Approach This

▶ Seven Steps

1 — Write the question. One sentence. Plain English. Before you open Power BI.

2 — Check your data. Does your data support that question? Apply what you learned in Lesson 3.

3 — Design before you build. Sketch what you want on paper. Where will the cards go? The charts? The slicer?

4 — Build. Open Power BI. Build your design. For charts, use the pattern from earlier lessons: choose what groups the data, choose what gets counted or measured, then choose the visual that answers the question best. Don't start over every time something isn't perfect — make decisions and move forward.

5 — Test with the meaning check. For every visual: read it as a sentence. If the sentence is wrong, fix it.

6 — Show someone before you present. Ask: "What does this tell you?" Make one adjustment based on their answer.

7 — Publish and present.

Final Validation

Before presenting:

  • Every chart answers a question.
  • Every card reads as a sentence.
  • Every visual has a plain-English title.
  • The slicer works.
  • The report is published.
  • Someone else has tested it.

The Presentation

Your presentation should answer four questions:

  1. 01What problem does this report solve?
  2. 02What does the most important number on the page mean — and why should anyone care?
  3. 03How does a reader use the slicer to focus on what matters to them?
  4. 04What would you build next if you had more time?

Five minutes maximum. No slides. Just the report and your voice.

Reflection After the Presentation

  1. 01What question did someone ask that you weren't prepared for?
  2. 02What would you change about the report now that you've seen someone else use it?
  3. 03What was the most useful thing on the page?
  4. 04What was the least useful?
  5. 05What question does your unit have that this report still can't answer?
That Last Question

The gap between what your report can answer and what your unit needs to know — that's the seed of Volume 2. Hold onto it.

Volume 1 Complete

Right now, Power BI is something you've already done. You've imported data, built charts and cards, filtered, formatted, published, shared, and presented. With real data. About a real question. For a real person.

That's not practice. That's the job.
