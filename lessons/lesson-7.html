Share What You See

Before This Lesson
A report lives on my computer. That's good enough.

Pattern #7

Publish, Test, Improve

Question: Does the report communicate once someone else uses it?

Recipe: Publish, test the link, share with one person, ask what the report tells them, then improve it.

Welcome Back

You've built a report. Charts. Cards. Labels. A slicer. Designed for a specific person. Answers a real question. And right now it's sitting on your computer.

Where no one else can see it.

Which means it hasn't done anything yet. A report that lives on your hard drive is like a briefing you gave to an empty room. Today we get it in front of the person it was built for.

Power BI Desktop vs Power BI Service

The Workshop and the Storefront

Power BI Desktop is the tool you've been using to build. Power BI Service is the web version — the place where you publish reports so others can access them.

Power BI Desktop is your workshop. Power BI Service is the storefront. You build in the workshop. You publish to the storefront. Your audience walks in through the storefront.

Step 1 — Sign In to Power BI Service

Getting There

Go to app.powerbi.com in your browser. Sign in with your work or school account. If you've never been here before, it will look empty. That's fine — by the end of this lesson it won't be.

Step 2 — Publish From Power BI Desktop

Publishing the Report

Go back to Power BI Desktop. Open the report you built in Lesson 6. At the top of the screen, find the Home ribbon. Click Publish.

If prompted, sign in with the same account. Choose My Workspace. Click Select. Wait for the upload to complete. When you see "Success," click the link to open your report in Power BI Service.

What Just Happened?

Your report left your computer. It's now on a server. Anyone you give access to can see it — from any browser, on any device. You didn't email a file. You didn't put it on a shared drive. You published it.

Before You Share

Open the report using the link yourself.

Verify the report loads.

Verify the slicers work.

Verify the charts display correctly.

Never assume a published report works until you've tested it.

Step 3 — Share With One Person

Sharing the Report

In Power BI Service, open your report. At the top right, click Share. Enter the email address of the person you designed this report for.

Write a short message that tells them: what question this report answers, what the slicer does, and what they should do if they have questions. Click Send.

The Ask — This Is Important

After you share it, ask that person one question: "What does this report tell you?" Not "do you like it?" Not "is it helpful?" — "What does this tell you?" Their answer reveals whether your report communicates what you intended. Write down exactly what they say.

Step 4 — Export to PDF

Sometimes your audience can't access Power BI Service. For those situations, you can export a static version. In Power BI Service, with your report open: click File → Export → PDF. Download the file.

This PDF is a snapshot. It won't update when the data changes. It won't respond to slicers. But for a briefing or a printed handout — it works. Know when to use which.

Drill #7

▶ Live vs Static

Publish your report to Power BI Service. Open it in the browser. Use the slicer — does it work the same way as in Desktop?

Export it to PDF. Open the PDF. Try to use the slicer. You can't — it's a snapshot. Understand the difference in your hands, not just your head.

Reflection

  1. 01What did the person you shared with say the report tells them?
  2. 02Did their answer match your intention?
  3. 03What would you change based on their response?
  4. 04When would you use a live report versus a PDF export?
  5. 05How does it feel different to see your report in a browser versus in Power BI Desktop?

Challenge

Share your report with a second person — someone different from the first. Ask the same question: "What does this report tell you?" Compare their answers. Did they understand the same things? Did one person catch something the other missed? What does that tell you about your design?

Teach Back

Explain the difference between Power BI Desktop and Power BI Service. Explain how to publish a report. Explain when to use a live report versus a PDF. Explain why "what does this tell you?" matters more than "do you like it?"

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1 — Sharing the Desktop file instead of publishing

Sending a .pbix file means they need Power BI Desktop installed to open it. Most people don't have it. Always publish to Power BI Service and share the link.

Mistake 2 — Assuming people know what the report is for

Always include a brief description in your share message. Tell the reader what question they should be asking while they look at it.

Mistake 3 — Never asking for feedback

Sharing without asking "what does this tell you?" means you'll never know if it worked. That question is not optional.

Mistake 4 — Forgetting to check access

When you share a report, the recipient needs access to both the report and the data behind it. If they see an error, the data source is likely not accessible to them. Troubleshoot methodically.

Homework

Take the feedback from the person you shared with. Make one meaningful change based on what they said. Publish the updated version. Send them a message: "I updated it based on your feedback. Does this version answer the question more clearly?" Then begin preparing for Lesson 8 — review everything you've built.

Lesson Summary

Before this lesson, a report lived on your computer.

After this lesson, a report is only finished when someone else can see it and understand it.
