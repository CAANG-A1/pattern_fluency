Different Pictures Tell Different Stories

Before This Lesson
A chart is a chart. They all show the same thing.
→
After This Lesson
Different charts answer different questions better.

Welcome Back

Last lesson, you turned your data into a picture.

But more importantly, you learned your first pattern: Group & Count.

You placed a category in the X-axis, placed a record identifier in the Y-axis, and Power BI counted how many records belonged to each category.

Today we discover something new: the chart itself is not the point. The question is the point. Different charts help answer different questions better.

Most people — including experienced analysts — pick charts out of habit. By the end of this lesson, you'll pick a chart because it answers your question better than the alternatives.

Before We Open Power BI

Think about two different questions you could ask about the same group of people:

Question A: Which unit has the most people? (A comparison — who's bigger.)

Question B: What percentage of the total force belongs to each unit? (A proportion — how much of the whole.)

Same data. Different question. Different picture.

Write down two questions you could ask about your own data before opening anything.

Remember Pattern #1?

Last lesson, you learned Group & Count.

That pattern answered this kind of question:

How many are in each category?

Today, you're going to discover a second pattern. Do not worry about the name yet. Just pay attention to what changes when the picture changes.

Step 1 — Open Your Work From Lesson 1

Open Power BI. Load the same file you used in Lesson 1. Build the same chart you built last time using Pattern #1:

Rebuild Pattern #1
  1. Select the chart icon that looks like vertical bars.
  2. Place Unit in the X-axis.
  3. Place Name in the Y-axis.
  4. Confirm that Power BI counts the names by unit.

You just rebuilt your first chart without walking through all of Lesson 1 again. That's Pattern #1 living in your hands now.

Observation — Watch Before You Touch

The Rule

You're going to change the chart type several times. The data will not change. The fields will not change. Only the picture changes.

Watch which question each picture answers easily — and which question it makes harder.

Step 2 — Change the Chart Type

Try Different Pictures

Click on the chart you just built.

In the Visualizations panel, click the Pie chart icon.

Same data. Same fields. Different picture.

Now try the Donut chart.

Then try the Table.

Then switch back to the Bar chart.

After each switch, pause and ask: What question does this picture answer best?

If the Chart Breaks

Don't panic. When you switch chart types, Power BI may move fields around or display something unexpected.

If that happens, switch back to the bar chart and check:

  1. Unit should be in the X-axis.
  2. Name should be in the Y-axis.
The Coach's Observation

The data never changed. The answer never changed. Only the way you viewed it changed.

You are not changing reality. You are changing perspective.

A chart is a lens. Different lenses reveal different things.

What You Just Learned

You may think you just learned how to switch chart types.

You didn't.

You learned your second Pattern Fluency pattern.

The data can stay exactly the same while the visual changes how clearly the answer appears.

Pattern #2

Change the Lens

Question: Which picture answers this question most clearly?

Recipe:

  • Keep the data the same.
  • Keep the fields the same.
  • Change the visual.
  • Notice what becomes easier to see.
  • Notice what becomes harder to see.

You are not changing reality. You are changing perspective.

Drill #2

▶ The Switching Drill
  1. Build a bar chart.
  2. Place Unit in the X-axis.
  3. Place Name in the Y-axis.
  4. Pause and answer your first question.
  5. Switch to a pie chart.
  6. Pause and answer your second question.
  7. Switch to a donut chart.
  8. Pause and ask what this picture makes easier or harder to see.
  9. Switch back to a bar chart.
  10. Delete the chart.

Repeat this sequence three times.

You're building Pattern #2, not memorizing chart types.

Reflection

  • 01Which chart felt most natural to read?
  • 02Which chart made your question hardest to answer?
  • 03Did switching chart types change how you thought about your data?
  • 04Which chart would you show to someone who had 10 seconds to understand the answer?
  • 05Does your choice feel right — or did you just pick the familiar one?

Challenge

Take your data. Write a third question you haven't asked yet. Build the chart you think answers it best.

  1. Put the category you want to group by in the X-axis.
  2. Put the field you want to count or measure in the Y-axis.
  3. Then switch chart types and compare what changes.

Does the new chart answer your question better or worse? Write down what you found.

Teach Back

Explain why the same data can look completely different. Explain Pattern #2 in plain language:

Keep the data the same. Change the picture. Notice what becomes clearer.

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1 — Thinking there's a best chart

There isn't. There is only a chart that answers a particular question better than others.

Mistake 2 — Choosing charts because they look impressive

Clarity beats aesthetics every time.

Mistake 3 — Forgetting the question

Write the question before you touch the chart. Check it after.

Mistake 4 — Never switching

Try at least two chart types before you commit.

Mistake 5 — Forgetting the field boxes

If a chart looks wrong after switching, check where Power BI placed the fields.

Homework

Create a new spreadsheet about something in your life outside work. Use at least 8 rows and 2 columns. Import it into Power BI. Write two different questions. Build the best chart for each question.
