Pattern #16

Build the Full System

Question: Can I move from a real unit need to a live, connected, decision-ready dashboard?

Recipe:

  • 1 — Name the decision (Pattern #13). One sentence. Who decides? What do they decide?
  • 2 — Read the shape (Pattern #3). What data exists? What's missing? Where are the gaps?
  • 3 — Connect the tables (Pattern #9). Find the linking columns. Build the relationships.
  • 4 — Close any gaps (Pattern #15). Missing calculation, column, or source? Fix it now.
  • 5 — Derive the measures (Pattern #11). Write the calculations in plain English. Translate to DAX.
  • 6 — Build the visuals (Patterns #1, #2, #5, #10, #12). Group & count. Trend. Cards. Drill-down. Use the right pattern for each question.
  • 7 — Design for the decision (Pattern #13). Must-see number at the top. Supporting context below. Remove everything else.
  • 8 — Keep it current (Pattern #14). Move data to SharePoint. Configure scheduled refresh.
  • 9 — Publish, test, improve (Pattern #7). Share. Ask "what does this tell you?" Fix what doesn't work.
  • 10 — Present. Five minutes. No slides. The dashboard and your voice.

The Capstone Requirements

Requirement 1 — A Real Decision

Name the specific decision your dashboard supports. One sentence. The person who makes it. What they decide. When they decide it.

Requirement 2 — Multiple Connected Tables

Your dashboard must draw from at least two connected data sources. The answer must require both — not be answerable by either alone.

Requirement 3 — At Least One Measure

At least one number on the dashboard must be a measure you created — not a column that already existed in the data. Write the plain English sentence for it before presenting.

Requirement 4 — Complete Construction

Your dashboard must include: at least two card visuals (including your measure), at least one trend or comparison chart, at least one drill-down hierarchy or slicer, plain English labels on every visual, and a title that states the decision — not the data.

Requirement 5 — Live Data

Your data must be on SharePoint or OneDrive for Business. Scheduled refresh must be configured. The dashboard must update without manual intervention.

Requirement 6 — A Verbal Presentation

Present to the person who makes the decision your dashboard supports. Five minutes maximum. No slides. Explain: what decision this supports, what the must-see number means, how the refresh works, and what you would add next.

How to Approach This

▶ Pattern #16 — Ten Steps

1 — Write the decision. Before anything else.

2 — Read the shape. List every data source you have. Label columns C or N. Identify gaps.

3 — Close the gaps. Missing calculation, column, or source? Fix it before building visuals.

4 — Connect the tables. Find the linking columns. Build the relationships in Model view.

5 — Write your measures in plain English. Before opening the measure editor.

6 — Create the measures in DAX. Test each one before using it in a visual.

7 — Build the visuals. Most important at the top. Supporting context below. Use the right pattern for each question.

8 — Move data to SharePoint. Configure scheduled refresh.

9 — Publish. Test. Share. Ask "what does this tell you?" Fix one thing.

10 — Present.

The Presentation

  1. 01What decision does this dashboard support — and who makes it?
  2. 02What is the must-see number — and what does it mean in plain language?
  3. 03What measure did you create — and what plain English sentence describes it?
  4. 04How does the dashboard stay current without manual work?
  5. 05What question does your unit still have that this dashboard cannot answer?

Five minutes maximum. No slides. The dashboard on the screen and your voice.

Reflection After the Presentation

  • 01Did the decision-maker make a better decision because of the dashboard? How do you know?
  • 02What was the hardest gap to close — and how did you close it?
  • 03What question does your unit have that still can't be answered?
  • 04What would Volume 3 need to teach you to close that final gap?
  • 05Who else in your unit could benefit from learning this — and are you ready to teach them?
